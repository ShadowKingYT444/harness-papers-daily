## One line
A Bayesian variance-decomposition study across 22 agent benchmarks shows that evaluation reliability depends on what you want the score to mean: fixed model-scaffold systems rank reliably, but model-only conclusions are much less stable, and adding more similar tasks cannot repair uncertainty caused by narrow scaffold coverage.

## The problem
Agent leaderboards mix several sources of variation: the model, the scaffold or harness, the task sample, and random run-to-run noise. A single score can therefore answer one question well and another question badly. The paper asks a more precise question than "is this benchmark reliable?": reliable for which inference?

## How it works
The authors build a Bayesian variance-decomposition framework for sparse and imbalanced leaderboards and apply it to 22 benchmarks from the Holistic Agent Leaderboard and Harbor Index. The framework separates signal from noise relative to the intended claim. They then project how reliability changes when evaluation budget is spent on more tasks or on broader benchmark coverage.

## Key results
Fixed model-scaffold systems have ranking reliability of 0.935-0.994, while underlying-model reliability ranges from 0.148 to 0.841. Scaffold choice can change model-ranking conclusions. When uncertainty is dominated by limited scaffold coverage, even infinitely many similarly constructed tasks improve model-ranking reliability by at most 0.097. Pooling diverse benchmarks raises projected cross-task reliability from 0.44 to 0.75 at the same task budget and can reduce projected cost by up to 83%.

## How it relates to adaptive decomposition
RHEvolution is changing the harness itself. During search, the object being compared is a model-plus-harness candidate, so harness variation is the treatment, not nuisance noise. But when the claim becomes "this evolved harness generalizes," variation across tasks, benchmarks, and models becomes essential evidence. The result on more tasks is also a warning against spending all compute on repeated examples from one benchmark. If the dominant uncertainty comes from benchmark or configuration coverage, more same-distribution tasks buy little.

## Limits and open questions
This paper studies leaderboard inference, not an online evolutionary optimizer. Its reliability quantities should not be copied directly as acceptance thresholds for RHEvolution without validation. The core lesson is about budget allocation and claim definition, not a ready-made selection rule.
