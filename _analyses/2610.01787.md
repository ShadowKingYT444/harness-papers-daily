## One line
The study separates experience into locators, procedures, state facts, and lessons, then tests which destination works best for each type.

## The problem
A full trajectory mixes information with different lifetimes and reuse patterns. Treating the full bundle as one unit can hide these differences.

## How it works
The authors compare the same components when supplied through context or through persistent learning across three backbone families, two environments, and three seeds. A routing rule uses recurrence and state-conditionality.

## Key results
Locators and lessons favor persistent learning, while procedures and state facts favor context in the reported settings. The routing rule recovers the preferred destination in 24 of 24 held-out cells. It beats every whole-trajectory baseline and improves by 3.5 points on average over the better single destination for each backbone.

## How it relates to adaptive decomposition
The important lesson for RHEvolution is that the unit of experience matters. A trace can be split before the system decides how to reuse it. Different parts can go to temporary context, long-term memory, reusable skills, or other persistent components. This supports decomposition before adaptation rather than treating each trajectory as one indivisible object.

## Limits and open questions
The four-part taxonomy is human-designed rather than discovered. The paper is evidence for routing after decomposition, not direct evidence that an optimizer can discover the right decomposition by itself.
