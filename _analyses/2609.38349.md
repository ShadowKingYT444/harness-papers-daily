## One line
MILO (Meta-evolutionary Island Orchestration) co-evolves the harness *and its own search strategy*: island-partitioned lineage memory that retains rejected mutations as negative evidence, per-island mutator agents that rewrite whole harnesses, and an orchestrator agent that diagnoses search stalls and intervenes by reassigning mutators, grafting lineages, speciating niches, or reshaping the task curriculum.

## The problem
Automated harness discovery is usually cast as LLM-driven evolutionary search, but existing methods fail in four ways: (1) **exploration** — LLM mutators exploit far more than they explore, collapsing onto variants of a few designs, while gains on long-horizon tasks come from global structural mutations; (2) **sample efficiency** — scoring one harness takes hours, yet most methods are failure-blind, keeping only survivors; (3) **self-adaptive search** — strategies are fixed a priori and can't escape local optima (only EvoX adapts, and only parent selection/mutation); (4) **multi-objective fitness** — almost all optimize accuracy alone and drift to costly harnesses; none optimize latency. MILO's key insight: evolve the complete search strategy (memory, mutators, curriculum) alongside the harness.

## How it works
Two coupled loops. The **inner loop** evolves harnesses per island: (1) select a parent from the island's admitted set; (2) the island's mutator agent rewrites the *whole harness* conditioned on the parent's failure traces/verifier reports and the island's lineage memory — which retains *rejected* candidates, so failures prune later mutations and push toward bolder structural changes; (3) score on accuracy, tokens, latency; (4) admit only Pareto-frontier advancers; (5) monitor held-out accuracy and a per-island stall counter. Islands grow in isolation from distinct seeds and exchange ideas but never compete for survival, confining each mutator's exploitative bias to its island. The **outer loop** fires when an island stalls beyond patience P: the **orchestrator agent** (which observes all islands) **diagnoses** the bottleneck as one of four — dry mutator (repeatedly rejected/inert children), exhausted lineage, crowded-out niche (a dominant lineage suppressing a distinct minority subtree), or whole-population plateau — then **intervenes** with up to two actions: *Reassign* (new mutator for the island), *Graft* (breed two islands' best harnesses as co-parents), *Speciate* (promote a suppressed subtree to its own island), or *Curriculum* (reshape the search task set toward high-regret tasks — tasks a few harnesses solve and most fail, where search helps most). The final harness is the one that most advances the accuracy–cost frontier on held-out tasks.

## Key results
- With Opus 4.8, MILO improves resolution rate over its initial harness by **+12.0% (Terminal-Bench 2.1), +28.3% (PaperBench), +10.3% (DeepSWE)** — vs best prior search gains of +4.5% (GEPA), +18.3% (Meta-Harness), and 0%.
- Terminal-Bench 2.1: **86.1±2.0%**, above the official leaderboard's top entry (83.8±2.3%), while consuming **26% fewer tokens** than its initial harness.
- With gpt-oss-120b: 15.6% on DeepSWE — **20× its initial harness**, while prior search methods stay below 1%.
- Generalization: the Terminal-Bench-evolved harness scores **2.7× Mini-SWE-Agent on the harder Frontier-Bench** with no further search.
- On EinsteinArena's open problems, MILO-evolved harnesses tighten best-known upper bounds: Erdős minimum-overlap 0.3808586→0.3808568, autocorrelation inequalities 1.50274365→1.50274360 and 1.45081→1.44889 — surpassing AlphaEvolve, TTT-Discover, and EvoX.
- Search-mechanism ablation (RR@5 on Terminal-Bench 2.1): flat LLM mutator 76.4 → +agentic mutator 79.1 → +lineage memory 79.3 → +islands 80.5 → **+orchestrator 86.1** — the largest single gain comes from the orchestrator. Even the weakest seed can yield the best harness.

## How it relates to adaptive decomposition
MILO is the closest *structural* cousin of adaptive decomposition in the current literature — it already implements three of its moves under different names. **Islands ≈ parallel component lineages** evolving without competing for survival; **Speciate ≈ split** (promote a distinct subtree solving different tasks into its own lineage); **Graft ≈ fuse/crossover** (breed two islands' best harnesses); **the orchestrator's diagnose→intervene loop ≈ the meta-controller** adaptive decomposition needs, detecting stalls and restructuring the search itself. The rejected-mutation memory is negative evidence as a first-class search input. The critical differences: (1) MILO evolves **whole harnesses**, never a decomposition the agent discovers — islands are a fixed population structure, not recursively split/fused components; (2) the orchestrator diagnoses *search stalls*, not *component-level credit assignment* — it never asks "which component caused this failure"; (3) no per-component search budgets and no monolithic-fallback logic. Adaptive decomposition can be read as MILO's orchestrator turned inward: instead of managing islands of whole harnesses, manage the decomposition itself — let the agent discover the split, budget each component's search independently, and fuse or revert based on local-vs-global agreement.

## Limits and open questions
No explicit limitations section; the honest gaps are structural. The orchestrator's four diagnoses and four interventions are a fixed action set — the search *strategy* adapts, but the *meta-strategy* (what diagnoses exist) is human-designed. Islands never compete, which preserves diversity but may waste budget on dead lineages; there's no mechanism to kill an island, only to reassign/graft/speciate around it. All results are on software-engineering and math benchmarks with two backbones; the generality of the orchestrator's diagnoses to other agent domains is untested. The multi-objective admission (accuracy/tokens/latency) is Pareto-gated, but the paper doesn't report how often latency actually trades against accuracy. Open: what happens if the unit of evolution is a *component* rather than a whole harness — do islands of components with an orchestrator managing the decomposition itself beat islands of monoliths? That's the experiment adaptive decomposition proposes.
