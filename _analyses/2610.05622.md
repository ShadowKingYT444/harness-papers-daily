## What is actually new

UndoBench separates **normal task competence** from **fault recovery**. It uses counterfactual paired trials with the same task and seed: one nominal run and one fault-injected run. It also records wire-level effects and final environment state.

This is useful for RHEvolution when a candidate changes retry, recovery, reconciliation, or compensation logic.

## Core mechanism

The key metric is conditional recovery success rate, or CRSR. It measures recovery only on trials that the system can solve in the nominal condition.

Faults are injected around five phases: before mutation, during mutation, after mutation but before acknowledgment, after acknowledgment but before checkpoint, and during compensation.

~~~mermaid
flowchart LR
 A["Task + fixed seed"] --> B["Nominal run"]
 A --> C["Faulted run"]
 B --> D["Competence"]
 C --> E["Wire effects + state"]
 D --> F["Condition on competent trials"]
 E --> F
 F --> G["CRSR + duplicate effects"]
 G --> H["Phase-aware owner mutation"]
 H --> I["Repeat paired test"]
~~~

## Experimental setup and results

UndoBench has **36 base workflows** and **36 fault scenarios** across **8 enterprise domains**. The split is 14 DEV, 10 VAL, and 12 TEST workflows.

The frozen lost-acknowledgment study uses two open-weight models, two frameworks, three recovery paradigms, and 20 seeds. This gives **2,880 paired trials** or **5,760 executions**.

Nominal competence is **83.54%**. Unconditional fault recovery success is **39.03%**. CRSR is **46.72%**.

Naive retry has CRSR **42.77%** and duplicate-effect rate **53.33%**. Idempotency keys reach CRSR **53.49%** with duplicate-effect rate **45.42%**. Local journal/reconciliation reaches CRSR **43.89%** with duplicate-effect rate **51.67%**.

The aggregate idempotency advantage is not statistically secure across task clusters. The reported clustered interval includes zero. The stronger result is that recovery quality depends on execution phase and workflow.

On five workflows without supporting idempotency or database constraints, naive retry produces duplicate executions in **400/400** tested cases.

## Why this matters for RHEvolution

A terminal pass score can reward a harness that repeats irreversible external effects.

For recovery-sensitive tasks, fitness should include nominal success, CRSR, duplicate-effect rate, unresolved recovery, and compensation correctness.

UndoBench also gives a split trigger. If one generic retry owner works before mutation but fails after commit-before-ack, RHEvolution should consider separating pre-mutation retry from post-commit verification/reconciliation and compensation.

## What RHEvolution should change

1. Add paired nominal/faulted runs for candidates that alter retry or side-effect handling.
2. Store execution phase in each failure record.
3. Reject candidates that improve task completion by increasing duplicate external effects.
