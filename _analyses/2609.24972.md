## One line
Applies ML-style regularization (L0/L1/L2-inspired) to recursive harness self-improvement to stop evolution from overfitting to the evolve set, so gains transfer to unseen benchmarks.

## The problem
Iterative harness evolution looks impressive on the split it's evolved against but the gains often shrink or vanish out-of-distribution — the search is adaptive empirical optimization over a highly expressive space, and it learns to exploit evolve-set quirks. Three coupled failure modes: benchmark-specific fitting (encoding task names/answers into the harness), noise chasing (stochastic winners become permanent), and complexity accumulation (bigger harness scores better without better mechanisms). Figure 1 shows prior methods ending *below* their starting harness on OOD splits.

## How it works
RRSI keeps the harness fully editable (prompts, control flow, tools, skills, memory, subagents) but regularizes both sides of the propose-select loop:
- **Proposal side**: (a) a temporally *annealed edit budget* b_t — early rounds can bundle several coordinated edits, later rounds get sparser (L0-style cardinality constraint on updates); (b) *evidence-aware credit assignment* — the proposer conditions on the full history of which components were modified, what hypothesis each tested, and whether it was accepted/rejected, so falsified hypotheses aren't re-spent; (c) *structured exploration* — when progress stalls (within the empirical noise band δ for w rounds), proposal budget is reserved for components never exercised, like entropy regularization.
- **Selection side**: (d) a *leakage critic* screens candidate diffs for benchmark-specific logic before evaluation (leaking candidates never get their inflated score); (e) a *noise-adjusted acceptance floor* — a candidate must beat the best-so-far minus the measured noise band δ (estimated by repeatedly evaluating the unchanged base harness); (f) *complexity-aware acceptance* — token-cost increase must be justified by score gain, ΔC ≤ β0 + β1·ΔS (Ridge/L2-style); (g) *structural pruning* — components with no recent positive contribution are reported as deletion targets (Lasso/L1-style sparsification).

## Key results
- Across 8 benchmarks in 3 domains (coding: Terminal-Bench 2.1, SWE-bench Verified; agentic workspace: Harvey LAB, JobBench, GDPval, APEX-Agents; engineering design: EngDesign, Frontier-Eng): up to 14.1 pp gain on the evolve split and up to 4.7 pp on five OOD benchmarks.
- It posts the *smallest* evolve-set gain of any evolved harness but the only OOD average that clears the base harness by >1 pp (43.6 vs 39.7) — the intended regularization trade.
- Runs on 30% fewer policy tokens than unregularized evolution.
- Outperforms the OOD average of the four prior baselines (Meta-Harness, AHE, TTHE, HarnessX) by up to 22.9%; all four baselines work well in-distribution but invert out-of-distribution.

## How it relates to adaptive decomposition
RRSI edits "component-wise" (prompts, control flow, tooling, memory, context management are the natural edit atoms), but the component taxonomy is fixed and the decomposition is never itself searched. Notably, its *structured exploration* already senses when the search has collapsed onto a narrow edit family and redirects budget to unexplored components — a primitive of adaptively allocating search attention, but at the level of a fixed part-list, not an evolving decomposition. There's no splitting of failing components into finer pieces, no fusing, and no monolithic revert: its safety valve is per-component pruning plus conservative acceptance. The annealing of the edit budget (many edits early → sparse edits late) is directionally similar to adaptive decomposition's "start monolithic, split failing parts" — except the granularity schedule is a fixed cosine curve, not driven by disagreement between local and global correctness.

## Limits and open questions
All the classical-regularization analogies (L0/L1/L2) are explicitly qualitative, not mathematical — they guide intuition but there's no unified theory; β0, β1 and the annealing schedule are tuned per setup. The leakage critic is itself an LLM reading diffs, so its reliability bounds the whole method. Eight benchmarks is decent but the three domains all evolve harnesses on a single suite; I'd want to see independent repeat runs with different seeds (the core evidence gap pattern) to know the regularization effect is stable rather than one lucky run.
