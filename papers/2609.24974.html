Harness-Zero: Harness Distillation via Agent-as-Harness

Haoran Ye, Yuxing Lu, Haonan Dong, Zhaochen Su, Guojie Song · published 2026-09-21 · arXiv:2609.24974

context

One line

Instead of keeping an evolved harness at deployment, it uses the optimized harness to guide a harnessing agent that reviews/corrects a student's responses, then SFTs the student on those reviewed trajectories so the harness's gains get internalized into weights — "harness distillation."

The problem

Harness optimization improves the external scaffolding, so its gains stay tied to that harness at deployment. But the best harness varies by domain, instance, and model — so you either settle for one suboptimal shared harness or maintain many specialized ones (routing cost, context cost, tool cost). Nobody had moved the discovered improvements into the model itself, partly because source and target harnesses differ in action space and available information, so source trajectories aren't valid supervision for the target.

How it works

Three stages: (1) Evolve & adapt — evolve a student-side harness h⋆ on training tasks (a skill-guided evolution loop), then adapt it into a private reference harness K for a separate harnessing agent, relative to the fixed target harness h (e.g., a middleware rule in h⋆ that blocks risky actions becomes a review-time warning in K triggered when the student proposes such an action). (2) Agent-as-harness collection — the student runs under the minimal target harness h (mini-SWE-agent with one Bash tool) and proposes response yt each turn; the harnessing agent intercepts it, consults K, and either PASSES it unchanged or REPLACEs it with the smallest coherent correction expressed as a complete response valid in h's action space; the accepted response executes through h. The rejected proposal and private review stay out of the student's trajectory; the harnessing agent can't see hidden solutions or the sandbox. (3) Train & deploy — SFT (2-epoch LoRA) on the reviewed trajectories, masking reviewer-perspective reasoning; at deployment the distilled student runs under h alone, with h⋆, K, and the harnessing agent removed.

Key results

  • Training-free: on frontier models, agent-as-harness with adapted K averages 81.1% across 6 benchmark–model settings vs 78.1% for code-as-harness (evolved harness mounted directly) and 68.6% for the minimal harness — the advantage can't come from a stronger model since the same model plays both roles.
  • Distillation (Qwen3.5-9B): macro-average 23.3% → 44.3% under the minimal harness, exceeding the 41.7% the base model gets with h⋆ still attached; 82.3% average recovery of 28 harness-induced behavior patterns.
  • Supervision quality: harness-guided review beats direct teacher trajectories (12.0% vs 30.0% on USPTO after SFT), review with empty K (11%), and even review with oracle answers (15% despite 98.6% collection success) — procedural guidance distills better than answer access.
  • On USPTO it trails h⋆ (30.0% vs 38.0%), suggesting deep domain knowledge is harder to internalize than procedures.

How it relates to adaptive decomposition

Tangentially. This paper moves along the model axis (harness → weights) rather than searching harness structure at all — the harness optimization that produces h⋆ is a simple off-the-shelf loop, not the contribution. There's no decomposition: the evolved harness is one DeepAgents-style abstraction, and distillation treats its behavior as a flat trajectory distribution to imitate. The one relevant resonance is the adaptation step Evolve→K→target-harness: translating mechanism guidance across different action spaces is structurally similar to what an adaptive decomposer must do when it splits a component — the subproblem's interface differs from the whole's. But the paper doesn't frame or solve that as a decomposition problem.

Limits and open questions

Distillation is demonstrated on a 9B student with a GPT-5.6-sol harnessing agent — the whole recipe depends on a much stronger reviewer than the student, and it's unclear what happens when the gap is small. USPTO results show knowledge-heavy harness behavior doesn't distill well. The three domains (spreadsheets, AppWorld, retrosynthesis) are all tool-use; nothing on coding agents or long-horizon agentic work. I'd probe scaling: how many reviewed trajectories are needed per behavior pattern, and whether the 82.3% recovery holds with the same model family as harnessing agent.
