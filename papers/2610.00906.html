ActiveSaddler: Automated Curriculum Learning for Agent Harness Optimization

Day 68 · Dec 06, 2026

ActiveSaddler: Automated Curriculum Learning for Agent Harness Optimization

Sungho Park, Wonjoong Kim, Jue Zhang, Wook-Shin Han, Pengfei Gao, Chanyoung Park, Yongqiang Yao, Rao Fu, Elsie Nallipogu, Qingwei Lin, Victor Rühle · published 2026-10-01 · arXiv:2610.00906

core mechanism

One line

Harness optimizers currently fix how the harness is updated but leave which training scenarios supply the evidence on a fixed schedule; ActiveSaddler turns scenario selection into an automated curriculum — a non-stationary bandit over dynamically instantiated failure-pattern arms — and gains +4.4 / +7.5 pp test Pass@1 on GAIA2 and Terminal-Bench 2.0 over the same optimizer with a fixed order.

The problem

Offline learning-based harness optimizers (e.g. AutoSaddler, GEPA) execute a predetermined set of training scenarios, diagnose deficiencies from the traces, and patch prompts/tool interfaces/control logic. As the harness evolves, two things shift that a fixed schedule cannot track: (1) unresolved failures may deserve further optimization while repaired ones deserve none, yet fixed schedules move on from unresolved failures too early or waste rollouts on dead ones; (2) the value of revisiting known failures vs. exploring unseen scenarios changes over optimization. Under a limited rollout budget both are costly — the authors show many failures observed during fixed-order training remain unresolved in the final harness.

How it works

ActiveSaddler wraps any learning-based offline harness optimizer and adapts the scenario set Xt at each iteration. Three components: (1) Failure-Pattern Extractor — converts failed execution records into failure-pattern arms; each arm represents an inferred harness weakness (not an individual trace or a fixed category), so related failures across scenarios pool evidence toward a shared optimization target. New arms are instantiated online as new patterns are diagnosed. (2) Arm Prioritizer — scores known arms by estimated potential for further learning progress (a priority φt per arm), so revisits target the weaknesses with the most remaining repair value. (3) Exploration Controller — at each iteration decides whether to explore unseen scenarios (discovering new failure patterns) or revisit a selected arm, adapting the exploit/explore balance as optimization proceeds. The outcome of each optimization step updates both arm priorities and the arm set, so the curriculum co-evolves with the harness. The paper instantiates it on AutoSaddler with fixed, disjoint train/dev/test sets.

Key results

On GAIA2 and Terminal-Bench 2.0, ActiveSaddler beats the same AutoSaddler optimizer with a scenario order fixed before optimization by +4.4 and +7.5 percentage points test Pass@1 respectively, reaching 59.8% and 80.0%. The curriculum also transfers: applying ActiveSaddler's curriculum to GEPA improves its average Pass@1 by +3.0 pp, showing the dimension is optimizer-agnostic. Ablations isolate the three design principles: replacing failure-pattern arms with coarse category arms costs 3.0/10.8 pp, scenario-level arms cost 3.6/6.7 pp; removing adaptive arm scoring costs 4.5/7.5 pp; replacing adaptive exploration with a fixed schedule costs 4.5/8.3 pp. Code and project page are released.

How it relates to adaptive decomposition

This paper adds the missing outer-loop data axis to harness self-improvement, and it rhymes strongly with adaptive decomposition. Failure-pattern arms are a discovered, coarse-grained decomposition of the harness's weaknesses — not hand-labeled categories, not single traces — which is the same granularity question the user's mechanism answers by splitting failing components. The insight that a fixed scenario order leaves failures unresolved while burning budget on dead ones is directly analogous to a fixed component decomposition: both need to adapt to where the learning signal is. Two reusable ideas: (a) the bandit's priority-as-estimated-learning-progress is a principled signal for where to spend the next optimization budget, which is exactly the decision the meta-controller makes when choosing split vs. fuse; (b) the finding that category arms are too coarse and scenario arms too fragmented is empirical evidence for the user's core claim that decomposition granularity must be discovered, not fixed. The contrast: ActiveSaddler never restructures the harness itself — it only schedules evidence. Adaptive decomposition restructures the structure; ActiveSaddler optimizes the data feeding the updates. The two are complementary, and a combined system (curriculum over scenarios × decomposition over components) is the obvious next paper.

Limits and open questions

The curriculum is only as good as the failure-pattern diagnosis: if the extractor conflates distinct weaknesses or fragments one weakness into several arms, the bandit allocates budget against a distorted map, and the paper's own ablations show arm granularity is load-bearing. Evaluation is two benchmarks with the same AutoSaddler optimizer as the harness-update engine, so the +7.5 pp is conditional on that optimizer being the bottleneck the curriculum can unlock. The exploration controller's balance relies on the estimated progress priorities, which are themselves computed from stale or stochastic evidence as the harness evolves — no convergence analysis is given. And like most AHO work, the train/dev/test split is human-curated and benchmark-bound; nothing shows the curriculum transfers to genuinely novel task distributions.
