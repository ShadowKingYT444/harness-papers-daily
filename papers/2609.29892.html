Qwen-Planner-Agent: A Closed-Loop AI-for-AI Framework for Real-World Mobile Planner Agents

Tingyu Qu, Weigao Sun, Yuecheng Liu, Yucheng Zhao, Yi Zhu, Yifeng Ding, Qiyi Wang, Sihan Cao, Pengkun Jiao, Hanlei Xie, Xiongwei Wu, Qichao Wang, Haodong Zhang, Jiajun Liu, Yuhao Wang, Yuqing Xie, Junpeng Zhao, Long Chen, Ming Ma, Sihan Yang, Ziwang Zhao, Yanhao Jia, Liangquan Gong, Feida Zhu, Yiran Zhong, Steven Hoi · published 2026-09-24 · arXiv:2609.29892

relevant

One line

An industrial "AI building AI" loop for mobile planner agents: agents build the training tasks and data, collect trajectories, curate them with feedback-driven refinement, train via SFT cold start + online agentic RL (with a competence-aware reward trick), and evolve the model and harness together from execution evidence — all human-gated and versioned.

The problem

Mobile planning is long-horizon and reliability-critical, but real-device interaction is expensive, which caps development scalability. The paper asks: can AI be both the object of development and an active participant in building the next system? The concrete bottlenecks are data (who constructs realistic long-horizon mobile tasks?), training (how to do online agentic RL without exploding reasoning/tool-use costs?), and the model–harness boundary (who refines the harness from execution feedback?).

How it works

Three interlocking loops under a shared action–feedback–verification contract. (i) AI for Data: a human-gated agentic data flywheel — specialized agents author executable tasks, collect interaction trajectories, curate and balance the training set, and use training feedback to guide subsequent data generation. (ii) AI for Training: supervised planning cold start, then hybrid-environment online agentic RL with CARE (Competence-Aware Reward-and-Advantage Engineering) — reward schedules and advantage signals adapt to the model's evolving competence to cut reasoning and tool-use costs while preserving performance. (iii) AI for Harness: a unified runtime orchestrating memory, skills, and tools; execution feedback and preserved failure traces feed AI-assisted diagnosis that proposes coordinated model and harness revisions. Key discipline: model and harness updates are reviewed and versioned offline (human gate), and model parameters stay fixed during serving. Development-set evaluation (excluded from training) guides data generation, training adjustments, and harness refinement across rounds.

Key results

  • Best overall performance among all evaluated models/systems on MobilePA-Bench (~77.8 overall in the reported figures), beating GLM 5.3, GPT 6 Astra, Claude Opus 5, Claude Fable 5, Qwen 3.8 Max, Kimi K3, Gemini 3.6 Flash, and Seed 2.1 Pro.
  • Improvements over its base model across tool use, memory, skills, and sub-agent coordination; further gains on non-mobile agentic benchmarks with general capabilities largely preserved.
  • Ablations support the effectiveness of model–harness co-evolution on both the model and harness side.

How it relates to adaptive decomposition

This is the industrial-scale version of model–harness co-evolution, and the "AI for Harness" loop is the closest thing here to an evolving harness — but the decomposition is fixed by humans (skills, memory, context, tools) and every revision passes a human review gate, so the agent never discovers the decomposition itself. No per-component search budgets, no split/fuse, no monolithic fallback — the structure is a designed system that gets refined, not a self-restructuring one. The CARE idea (adapting the optimization pressure to the agent's current competence) is a neat analog to adaptive decomposition's budget allocation by component difficulty, but applied to reward shaping rather than structural search.

Limits and open questions

The human review/approval gate is the scalability bottleneck — "AI for AI" still leans on people for every revision. Model params are fixed during serving, so there's no online adaptation. The paper doesn't quantify how much of the gain comes from data vs. RL vs. harness revisions. Open: does the co-evolution loop keep working as tasks drift, or does the flywheel need human re-anchoring? And what breaks first when the harness revisions are wrong — is there a rollback mechanism?
