Harness Annealing: Learning to Act with Less External Control

Day 74 · Dec 12, 2026

Harness Annealing: Learning to Act with Less External Control

Yingxuan Yang, Huacan Chai, Ying Wen · published 2026-10-01 · arXiv:2610.01235

highly relevant

One line

The mirror image of harness optimization: instead of improving the harness, teach the model to internalize it — Harness Annealing Training (HAT) combines explicit control supervision (Layered supervision, segmented by control layer rather than flat trajectory SFT) with a curriculum over teacher trajectories collected under progressively weaker harnesses, and annealed 9B/35B checkpoints act with tools-only nearly as well as their starting checkpoints acted with the full harness.

The problem

Harnesses supply not just tools and information but control decisions: what to investigate, whether to revise, when to stop. Training on successful harness-supported trajectories improves task performance while leaving those decisions dependent on runtime intervention — the model still needs the harness for judgments it could in principle learn. The paper asks whether harness-supported experience can teach the model to assume those control responsibilities, changing the model–harness division of control as the model learns.

How it works

HAT has two ingredients. (1) Explicit control supervision: Layered supervision segments trajectories by control layer (what to investigate / whether to revise / when to stop) rather than supervising flat token sequences, so the model is explicitly trained on the harness's control judgments. (2) Stronger-to-weaker curriculum: continued training introduces demonstrations collected under progressively weaker source harnesses (H4 full → H1 tools-only), with replay and cue dropout to prevent forgetting, so the model learns to act as external support is withdrawn. Experiments train 9B and 35B Qwen3.5 models and evaluate every checkpoint under all four deployment harnesses on SWE-QA and SWE-QA-Pro.

Key results

For 9B, the H2-targeted annealed checkpoint improves tools-only (H1) scores from 71.99 → 77.00 on SWE-QA and 74.64 → 77.73 on SWE-QA-Pro (+5.01/+3.09 pp) relative to its H4-trained starting checkpoint, while shrinking the cross-harness performance range — i.e. less dependence on support. Selected annealed checkpoints operating with tools alone score close to their starting checkpoints operating with the full harness. Layered supervision transfers better under weak support than Direct (trajectory-level) SFT — e.g. 9B Layered H1-trained reaches 72.48–77.52 under H1 deployment where Direct H1-trained collapses to 32.45–34.74. Benefits vary with model scale and deployment config: annealing does not uniformly improve everything.

How it relates to adaptive decomposition

This paper runs the harness-optimization arrow in reverse, and that's exactly why it matters for the user's mechanism. Adaptive decomposition assumes the harness is the thing that evolves while the model is fixed; HAT shows the division of labor itself is a variable — control responsibilities can migrate from harness into model weights. The honest implication: the optimal decomposition is not just "which components does the harness have" but "which responsibilities live in the harness vs. the model," and that boundary can be annealed. Practically, the Layered supervision idea (supervising control decisions by type rather than flat trajectories) gives the user's meta-controller a way to attribute which control responsibility a failing harness component was supposed to provide — a finer credit signal than success/failure. The strategic contrast: Co-Harness and SIA co-evolve weights and harness jointly, while HAT deliberately internalizes harness function into the model; the user's adaptive decomposition could use HAT as a "fuse into the model" operator's training recipe — when a component is consistently reliable, anneal it into the model and delete it from the harness.

Limits and open questions

The authors are candid: two Qwen3.5 models, one fixed harness hierarchy, a single training seed, and repository-QA as the main domain (plus one banking transfer experiment) — transfer to other model families, domains, and harness designs is open. Later checkpoints receive more training with different data mixtures, and without equal-budget controls the gains can't be attributed to annealing order alone; replay and cue dropout contributions are unseparated. Direct vs. Layered supervision are not matched for supervised-token counts or optimizer updates, so that comparison is between two training recipes, not cleanly about trajectory segmentation.
